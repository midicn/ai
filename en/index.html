AI Music

Making finished music with AI — tool choice, prompt to deliverable, controllability, hand-off and licensing. Sound itself is explained on prod; theory on theo.

Tools and Selection

  • Choosing Tools for a Sample PackA sample pack sells licensing and metadata, not file count.
  • Choosing Tools for RestorationRestoration stops at the source material, not the software; decide how far to go before choosing a tool.
  • Choosing a Tool for a Vocal DemoWhether the voice is yours, borrowed or synthesized decides the whole route for a vocal demo.
  • Choosing Tools for Game AudioLoops, layers and runtime behaviour decide a game audio route far more than timbre.
  • Choosing a Tool for a Film CueThe picture sets the hit points; the tool only decides what you fill them with.
  • Choosing a Tool for a Song DemoThree versions by tonight or one that can be edited note by note decides the route, not the software.
  • Tool Churn and Lock-inTools turn over every few months; keep material and sessions in common formats and no platform owns your work.
  • Choosing a ToolDo not ask which tool is best; write down your deliverable, control needs and rights limits first.
  • API versus AppAn app gives a ready workflow and an API gives automation; choose by how often you repeat the job.
  • Cloud versus Local ModelsCloud saves hardware and iterates fast; local keeps privacy and works offline. The trade is data and cost.
  • AI Inside the DAWGenerated material only joins the workflow once it is in the DAW, and format decides how much can be changed.
  • AI Mastering ToolsAutomatic mastering returns a listenable result fast, but loudness targets and dynamics remain human calls.
  • Voice Cloning ToolsCloning a voice is technically mature; the real questions are consent and use.
  • MIDI Generation ToolsGetting MIDI rather than audio means every note is editable, and every sound is your problem.
  • Stem Separation ToolsPulling vocals, drums and bass out of a mix opens up rework and study, and leaves residue behind.
  • Text-to-Music ModelsA sentence in, a song out feels magical, but it is probabilistic sampling plus human selection.
  • A Map of the ToolsAI music is not one big tool but a chain of small ones, each owning one stage.

Prompting and Workflow

  • From Idea to MasterA repeatable pipeline delivers more reliably than any single trick.
  • Prompt TemplatesSave prompts that repeatedly work as templates and change only the variables per job.
  • Writing Lyrics for AIWrite lyrics for singing, where stress, breath and rhyme decide whether a synthetic voice can deliver them.
  • Section-by-Section GenerationLong pieces rarely survive one generation; prompt section by section and stitch, or the form collapses.
  • Reference Audio ConditioningOne reference clip pins timbre and groove better than ten adjectives.
  • Iterative RefinementOne generation is luck; iteration is method, changing one variable at a time and keeping the last version.
  • Negative PromptsSaying what you do not want often works better than repeating what you do.
  • Tempo, Key and FormGive tempo as a number, key by name and form as sections; "flowing" buys no structure.
  • Describing InstrumentationSay who plays, how they play and how much space they take, which beats saying "rich".
  • Genre Words and ReferencesGenre words are shortcuts and traps; a reference track is more precise than a genre label.
  • The Anatomy of a PromptA good prompt reads like an arrangement brief: style, instrumentation, tempo, mood and form.

Generation and Editing

  • Seeds and ReproducibilityLog the seed and every parameter, but do not expect two runs to match exactly.
  • Controlling RhythmGroove lives in accents and micro-timing; a quantised grid flattens it into a ruler.
  • Controlling HarmonyWrite the progression out, then check the result against its bass line and inversions.
  • Controlling ArrangementDensity comes from assigning register and roles, not from stacking more layers on top.
  • Fixing ArtefactsTell a generation artefact from a performance choice, then decide whether to repair or regenerate.
  • Quantizing Generated PartsQuantise only the notes that miss the grid; a blanket pass removes the playing along with the error.
  • MIDI Post-EditingCorrect pitches are only the start; velocity, microtiming and note length make it sound played.
  • Stem-Level EditingSeparated stems are approximations, so every change to one is a change to the sum.
  • Extending and ContinuingContinuation rarely fails at the first note; it fails where the form has to decide what comes next.
  • Inpainting and EditingRegenerating one bar instead of everything is easy to ask for and hard to make seamless.
  • Generating VariantsGenerate many, choose fast, then refine only the winner; the comparison is the real work.

Mixing and Mastering

  • Common Mix ProblemsDull, harsh, unstable, pumping, flat, five symptoms and the first move for each.
  • Matching a ReferenceA reference is a ruler, not a template, so match the level and compare one dimension at a time.
  • Space and ReverbWhen the material already carries space, decide whether you want one room or two before adding reverb.
  • Stereo Field and WidthWidth is not depth, and a field widened by generated reverb has to survive the mono sum first.
  • Choosing a Dynamic RangeAI material is born flat, and how much contrast to keep is an aesthetic call rather than a setting.
  • Mastering AI-Generated MaterialAutomated mastering can fix level and brightness but cannot judge structure, breathing or intent.
  • Loudness TargetsMatch levels before comparing, and let platform targets and true peak decide how much dynamics you can keep.
  • Bus ProcessingGlue and saturation can flatter AI material, but compressing an already flat mix only makes it flatter.
  • Balance and MaskingGenerated parts fight over the same register first, so fix register and density before reaching for EQ.
  • Mixing AI StemsWhen the stems come from AI, the order of work has to change before the first fader moves.

Vocals and Sound

  • Preserving Human ExpressionThe more precise the machine, the less human it sounds; keep breaths, rubato and small errors on purpose.
  • Hybrid Human and AI PerformanceWith a human performance plus generated parts, decide who owns the beat before discussing tone.
  • Sample GenerationAfter generating one-shots and loops, recheck length, tuning, tail and rights.
  • Sound Design with AIGenerating texture is easy; making it usable means passing the mono and level checks first.
  • Choosing Instrumental TimbresSampled, synthesized and generated timbres each have a job; the wrong choice is a casting error, not a quality one.
  • Lyric IntelligibilitySynthetic vocals usually fail at being understood, and the fault lies in consonants and word endings.
  • Doubling and HarmonyDoubling thickens one voice, but a third copy starts fighting itself in phase.
  • Tuning VocalsTuning corrects pitch and timing; overdone, it removes vibrato first and humanity second.
  • Consent for Voice CloningBefore cloning a voice, put purpose, term, withdrawal and labelling into a record you can show.
  • Synthetic VocalsSynthetic singing handles demos, backing and languages you cannot record; long notes and emotion still give it away.

Film and Games

  • Music for Short FormNo hook in the first two seconds means no viewer, and the loop must survive repeats
  • Delivering to the EngineSample rate, loop points, loudness and naming, one mistake means a redo
  • Interactive LayersAn engine needs layers that stand alone and can be switched at any bar
  • Localization and DubbingSynthetic dubbing must pass timing, stress and pronunciation, then consent
  • Sound Effects GenerationA generated sound that seems right may still fail on transients, tails and mono
  • Sync and Cue EditingCut at the end of a phrase, never on an accent or inside a reverb tail
  • Trailer Music with AITrailers run on silence and hit points; generated cues must be re-cut to them
  • Adaptive Music with AILayering and resequencing only work when tempo, key and bar lines are shared
  • AI for Game AudioA game needs loopable, triggerable assets, not a finished song
  • AI for Film ScoringAI can draft temp scores and short cues; long arcs and themes still need a composer

Rights and Ethics

  • AI Contract ChecklistA working checklist covering scope, disclosure, rights, revisions and exit
  • Licensing AI OutputLicense the part you actually made, and keep uncertain rights out of the contract
  • Musician LivelihoodWhat gets displaced is rarely composition but session work, library cues and demo vocals
  • Compute and EnvironmentGeneration costs compute, and at scale both the invoice and the environmental bill arrive
  • Dataset BiasCorpora lean toward Anglophone pop and European classical, and output treats the lean as normal
  • Style MimicryLearning a set of devices is one thing; selling a living artist's name is another
  • Attribution and DisclosureCredit says who did what, disclosure says how; both should be short and specific
  • Platform TermsTerms are not about features but about what you upload, take away and leave behind
  • Voice and Likeness RightsA voice points to a specific person; get consent you can show before using it
  • Copyright in AI OutputA generated track may not belong to you; the human choices are what you can claim
  • Training Data and ConsentWhat a model learned shapes what you can do with it, and who should have been asked

Evaluation and Delivery

  • When Not to Use AISome jobs are faster, better or only legal with a human.
  • Archiving the WorkArchive so a re-edit, a licence or a claim is still possible years later.
  • ReproducibilityWhat reproduces is the process and the decisions, not the waveform.
  • Documenting the ProcessLog tool, version, prompt, seed and edits so the result can be defended.
  • Client ReviewThree options per stage, structured notes, and revisions with a boundary.
  • Versioning AI ProjectsA version is a prompt, a seed and a set of edits, not just an audio file.
  • Metadata and CreditsMetadata is the identity of a recording; credits and disclosure must be specific.
  • Files and StemsA delivery package exists so someone else can continue the work.
  • The Quality GateA pre-delivery gate sorted by cost to fix, not by another listen.
  • Blind Listening TestsA blind test needs three things, no labels, matched levels, random order.
  • Judging a GenerationJudge in order, form first and mix last, and ignore what post can fix.

Model Profiles

  • Lyric AssistantsIt offers candidates by theme, rhyme and form; singability and tone remain a human call.
  • Orchestration AssistantsIt spreads a piano sketch across sections, saving copying time while balance remains an ear decision.
  • Symbolic MIDI GenerationIt writes notes rather than audio, editable and transposable, but the structure is yours to finish.
  • The MusicLM Research LineA research line from audio continuation to text-to-music, where the method and its limits matter more than any product.
  • Adobe Enhance SpeechOne click removes room sound and noise, bringing phone recordings up to broadcastable quality.
  • iZotope AssistantsAssistants inside repair, mixing and mastering that turn judgement into suggestions you can edit.
  • LALAL.AI Online Stem SeparationA web separation service that splits a track into vocals, backing and individual instruments after upload.
  • Demucs-Family Stem SeparationAn open-source separation family that splits vocals, drums, bass and other offline, in batches.
  • Singing Synthesis: VOCALOID and Synthesizer VYou draw notes and type words to get a singing voice; pitch is exact, diction and expression are hand-worked.
  • DiffSingerOpen-source singing synthesis built on your own voicebanks and phoneme labels.
  • ElevenLabs Music and SoundEffects, dubbing and short music from an API-shaped audio service.
  • ACE StudioLyrics and a melody become singing, with pitch curves you can draw.
  • Google MusicFX / LyriaAn experimental entry for instrumental beds and effects; vocals are not its job.
  • RiffusionLoops made from spectrograms, useful for lo-fi and experimental material.
  • MusicGenOpen weights you can run locally, short clips steered by a melody.
  • Stable AudioInstrumental beds, loops and effects with a duration you can set.
  • UdioVocal timbre flatters, but section joins are where it gives itself away.
  • SunoA whole song with vocals in one pass, enough to judge direction before production work.

AI Tools

  • API PipelinesWiring generation into batch work saves repetition and owes you versions, cost and reproducibility.
  • AI Inside Game Audio MiddlewareAdaptive music and batch assets enter middleware, where the real difficulty is runtime and reproducibility.
  • Scoring Assistance for PictureGenerating temp scores and variants against picture is fast for direction, slow for conforms and clearances.
  • Sample GeneratorsText or audio prompts produce material for the sampler quickly, but rights and usability come first.
  • Synthetic Vocals in the SessionBringing synthetic voice into the session means clearing three gates: diction, expression and rights.
  • Automated Mastering ServicesUpload a mix and get a releasable master: fast and consistent, but it cannot rescue a mix.
  • Stem Separation Inside the DAWSplitting a finished mix into separately treatable tracks inside the session starts with whether the artefacts ruin this version.
  • Notation and Audio-to-ScoreTurning a performance into an editable score saves engraving time, but proofreading still happens bar by bar.
  • AI Plug-ins for Separation, Tuning and MasteringSeparation, tuning and mastering assistants inside the host buy speed, at the price of format and licence constraints.
  • AI Features Built into DAWsGeneration, alignment and harmony suggestions now live inside the session, saving round trips but not judgement.

Work You Can Finish

  • Delivering to a ClientDelivery is a checkable list of files, not a single mp3.
  • A RemixGet the stems and the licence before touching the vocal's time and pitch.
  • Long-Form AmbienceTwenty minutes without an event; the loop seam and the licence are the hard parts.
  • A Brand Sonic LogoThree to five seconds must be memorable and still work on a phone speaker.
  • A Sample FlipClear the sample before chopping; packs and old records follow different rules.
  • Multilingual VersionsOne backing track, lyrics rewritten to the melody, and a native check per language.
  • A Cover VersionComposition and master licences are separate; changing lyrics makes it a different job.
  • Scoring a Short FilmA cue sheet comes before the music; silence and dialogue placement decide the result.
  • A Game LoopLoop points, layers and state changes break more often than the music itself.
  • Background Music for VideoBackground music has to stay out of the way and survive ten minutes of repeats.
  • A Podcast IntroAn intro is a weekly template; clear speech matters more than a pretty bed.
  • A Thirty-Second AdAd music must hook in two seconds and survive cuts to fifteen and six.
