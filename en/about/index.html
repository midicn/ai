About AI Music

What this site covers, what it deliberately leaves out, and how to read it.

What this site is for

One question only: how to actually finish a piece of music using AI.

Between "I have a vague idea" and "I hand over a usable file" sit a chain of decisions — which tool, how to describe what you want, how to fix a generation that isn't right, what format to deliver in, and whether you may use the result at all. Those are questions of process and judgement, not acoustics.

Where it stops and Production begins

The same topic appears on both sites, each covering its own half:

Production & Digital Audio = how a sound is made (parameters, physics, protocols) This site = how a deliverable is made (tools, selection, workflow, control, hand-off, rights)

Compression, for example: that site explains why threshold and ratio make a part feel tighter; this one explains whether to compress a muddy full AI bound at all, and what to do with it afterwards. The two pages link and do not overlap.

Handling time-sensitive material

Tools here change every few months, so:

  • Entries about principles and judgement (controllability, how to evaluate, how rights stack up) carry no version numbers and stay true for years.
  • Entries about specific tools show an updated date and cover only what the tool is good at and where it bites — never click-by-click steps. Steps rot; judgement doesn't.
  • Prices and free-tier limits are never stated as facts, only where to check.

What this site does not do

  • No music theory — that lives on the theory and harmony site.
  • No sound fundamentals — sample rate, dynamics and filters are on Production.
  • No buying advice — comparison dimensions are given; "which is best" is not.
  • No promise that examples stay playable — AI-generated audio has a different rights position from synthesised demos; each entry labels its own.
